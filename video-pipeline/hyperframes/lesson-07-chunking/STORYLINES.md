# Lesson 7 — Chunking documents the right way: storylines

Source: `src/lib/modules/m3.ts`, lesson `chunking`. Every claim below comes from that lesson. The placement-policy
sentences and the comparison dots are examples and are shown as illustrative.

**The one idea:** the model only sees the chunks you retrieve, so how you cut the document decides the answer.
**Remembered as:** "Bad chunking ruins good models. Start at 500 tokens with 50 overlap, then measure."

Assumptions for all three: 1920×1080, ~90 s, ElevenLabs narration, no music (voice only, as in lesson 1's build).

---

## A. The sentence that has to survive (recommended — one answer sentence carries every idea)

- **Device / form:** the worked example; one frame that rebuilds.
- **What you see and feel:** Riya asks her college placement bot about backlogs. The answer is one sentence on
  page 4 of a 40-page policy, marked in coral. We follow that single sentence as the document is cut too big,
  too small, straight through the middle, and finally with overlap, so the viewer worries about the sentence and
  feels the relief when it comes through whole.
- **First 2 seconds:** a phone chat with Riya's question already sent, next to a policy page whose one coral
  sentence is the answer.
- **Beats:** question + the sentence on page 4 → the model only sees retrieved chunks → too large (many topics,
  noisy, filler) → too small ("It must be cleared…", what is "it"?) → ~500 tokens, but a fixed cut slices the
  sentence → overlap of ~50 tokens keeps it whole; split on sentences → ID card (file, page) becomes the citation →
  measure three ways → the one line.
- **Look:** the site's brand: mist ground, white cards, deep-teal structure, coral only on Riya's sentence and the
  cut that threatens it; Inter for words, JetBrains Mono for document text.
- **Sound:** warm, unhurried narration only.

## B. The paper cutter

- **Device / form:** the object is the explanation; a flight along one machine.
- **What you see and feel:** a long printed scroll of the policy runs under a guillotine blade. The blade spacing
  changes (huge sheets, confetti, then even sheets that tape over each other), and each sheet gets a stamped tag.
  Feels like a print-shop demo.
- **First 2 seconds:** a blade drops through a line of printed text and the two halves slide apart.
- **Beats:** scroll → giant sheets → confetti → blade through a sentence → taped overlap → page stamp → three
  stacks compared → the one line.
- **Look:** warm paper on a teal cutting mat, coral blade edge.
- **Sound:** narration with a paper-cut sound on each slice.

## C. The open-book exam (the bold one)

- **Device / form:** a moment in a student's day; split screen to one.
- **What you see and feel:** an exam hall. One student has the whole textbook dumped on the desk and drowns;
  another has torn scraps that make no sense; the third has neat, tabbed, overlapping notes and finds the answer.
  The three desks collapse into the third.
- **First 2 seconds:** a question paper lands on a desk beside a 40-page booklet: "How many backlogs are allowed?"
- **Beats:** three desks → whole book (noisy) → scraps (no context) → tabbed notes with overlap → page tabs as
  citations → the marks sheet (measure) → the one line.
- **Look:** exam-hall desks from above, teal tabs, coral answer underline.
- **Sound:** narration over quiet exam-hall room tone.
